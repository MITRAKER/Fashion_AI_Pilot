# Draping

Advisory reasoning about the three-dimensional route to a pattern. Like
[[fashion-patternmaking]], this does not produce production-authoritative output in the
pilot (PRD §1.2).

## Drape or draft?

| Drape it | Draft it |
|---|---|
| Silhouette depends on how the cloth falls | Silhouette is defined by seam geometry |
| Bias, cowls, soft gathers, asymmetric wrapping | Tailored, structured, engineered fit |
| Volume that needs to be *seen* to be judged | Repeatable variation on an existing block |
| No existing block is close | A block already fits the category |

Most real garments are a hybrid: drape the expressive part, draft the rest, then true the
whole thing flat.

## Principles

**The form is not the customer**
- A dress form is a standardized approximation. Padding out to a fit model or to the brand's
  own measurements is what makes the drape mean anything for that brand's customer.
- Mark the form's key lines — center front, center back, bust, waist, hip, princess lines,
  shoulder — before draping. Everything is measured against them.

**Grain governs behavior**
- Straight grain (warp) is the most stable; cross grain has slight give; true bias (45°)
  has the most stretch and the most fluid fall.
- Bias garments elongate under their own weight. They must hang — commonly 24–48 hours —
  before hemming, or the hem will drop unevenly after the customer wears it once.
- Center front and center back are placed on grain deliberately. Where the grain sits
  is a design decision, and it must be recorded, because the factory cannot infer it.

**Fabric hand cannot be substituted**
- Draping in muslin when the garment is chiffon tells you almost nothing about the final
  drape. Muslin is right for structured shapes; for fluid ones, drape in the real fabric
  or in something matched for weight, drape coefficient, and recovery.
- Weight, drape, stretch, recovery, and shrinkage are the five properties that decide
  whether the drape survives production.

**From drape to pattern**
- Mark seamlines, grainlines, notches, and balance points on the cloth *on the form*.
- Trued flat, the pieces must satisfy the same seam-mate rules as a drafted pattern.
  A drape that will not true is not yet a pattern.

## Validation rules this skill contributes

| Check | Severity |
|---|---|
| Bias-cut piece with no hang-time instruction in construction notes | Blocker |
| Grain placement unspecified on a drape-derived piece | Blocker |
| Fabric drape/weight incompatible with the intended silhouette | Warning; blocker when deterministic |
| Draped silhouette specified without a fit-model or form reference | Warning |
